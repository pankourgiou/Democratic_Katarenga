Democratic Katarenga

Real Katarenga rules, but nobody decides alone. You vote from a multiple-choice ballot proposed by your seven advisors. The council of seven advisors votes on every move for the opponent, using the voting program you pick.

Katarenga, as implemented

  • 8×8 board, 16 squares of each colour (four random quarterboards, 4 of each colour apiece). 8 pawns each on your baseline. You are ivory at the bottom.
  • A pawn moves by the colour of the square it starts on: red = rook, yellow = bishop, green = knight, blue = king.
  • Rook and bishop slide any distance but not beyond the next square of the same colour in that line (they may land on it). They cannot jump pawns.
  • You capture by landing on an enemy pawn. On your first move you may not capture a pawn on its home rank.
  • Reach the opposing baseline, then on a later turn step off the edge into one of the two enemy camps (corners). Pawns on that baseline can still be captured, or can move back into play.
  • You win by getting a second pawn into the other camp, or by capturing 7 enemy pawns.

The democratic layer

  • On your turn your advisors propose moves. You vote for one (or write in any legal move).
  • On its turn the council proposes, debates (advisors can change their minds), then the chosen program decides.
  • Advisors differ: Hawk attacks, Guardian protects, Surveyor wants mobility, Weaver keeps shape, Pioneer races for camps, Trickster is erratic, Pragmatist balances.

The real game ships fixed printed quarterboards; here they are randomised. Edge case not in the rules: a side with no legal move passes.
